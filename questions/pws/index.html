The responses, at a glance

The questions concentrate on the conditions needed to size and run reviews: package volume and class mix, furnished tools, access lead times, review authority, and how conflicts will be handled. Acquisition preferences diverge: several submissions advocate small-business set-asides, while Noblis expressly favors full and open competition. Answers that say “no questions” do not establish agreement with every provision.

Planning assumptions drive the questions

ACE, KITC, and Guidehouse ask for workload and operational boundaries rather than offering a single staffing estimate. Their questions connect throughput commitments to package complexity and dependencies outside contractor control.

Read the evidence:Advanced Cybersecurity Experts, LLC (ACE)Kapoor IT Consulting LLC (KITC)Guidehouse Inc.

A real acquisition disagreement

Assurit and Prudent advocate small-business set-asides. Noblis instead calls for full and open competition, arguing that existing program experience matters. These are competing acquisition preferences, not findings about vendor capability.

Read the evidence:AssuritPrudent Technology LLCNoblis, Inc.

Different ways to buy the work

Navira proposes a mix of fixed monthly management, per-review prices, and labor-hour surge. EY favors performance-based fixed-price units. Both seek predictable delivery, but allocate workload and cost risk differently.

Read the evidence:Navira FederalErnst & Young LLP

What does a reviewer actually do?

TES, Excentium, IBSS, and EY ask where PMO review ends and independent assessment or direct testing begins. This boundary also affects OCI screening and staffing.

Read the evidence:The Enterprise Security Consultants, LLC (dba TES Consultants)Excentium, Inc.International Business Sales And Services Corp (IBSS Corp)Ernst & Young LLP

Technical notes

Separate from agreement among respondents. Checked through the FedRAMP MCP on November 5, 2026, against dated source evidence. Scope and historical limitations.

Potentially inconsistentJSON SDR requirements are not an OSCAL mandate

CVP and the later CyberStorm submission refer to required or mandatory OSCAL components for the SDR. SDR-CSO-FRR instead specifies a human-readable SDR and JSON using the linked FedRAMP SDR schema. These responses need to distinguish a proposed tool capability from the required package format. The draft PWS was not available through MCP, so no conclusion is made about additional procurement-specific formats.

Applies to: 20x Program path, Classes B–D; initial certification effective July 4, 2026. Ongoing maintenance and grace provisions are separate.

Responses:Customer Value Partners, LLCCYBERSTORM DEFENSE (11/2/2026)

SDR-CSO-FRR: FedRAMP Rules · Published rules 2026.10.13.01

SupportedGenerative output is not deterministic telemetry

Prudent’s distinction is supported by FRD-DTM: generative or probabilistic output is not a factual record of system state and must not generate deterministic telemetry. This does not, by itself, prohibit approved drafting or analysis assistance.

Applies to: Definition in the June 24 rules, preceding all submissions.
