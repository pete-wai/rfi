Conflicts of interest

Before beginning a review, how would you identify organizational conflicts of interest involving your company, affiliates, employees, or subcontractors?

35 submissions · Compared within this question

Summary & comparisonTechnical notesCompany answers

The responses, at a glance

A recurring pattern is to compare package parties with company records and personnel disclosures, disclose potential conflicts, and use recusal or Government-approved mitigation. The differences matter: proposed lookbacks range from two to five years, some firms explicitly exclude their own offerings, and others emphasize managed separation of teams. These are proposed practices; they do not establish compliance with an unavailable PWS.

Shared process, different lookbacks

Cloud9 specifies 24 months, TES and RADIX propose three years, and FITS maintains a five-year customer list while leaving the cooling-off period to agreement. These are different screening proposals, not a single established requirement.

Quzara expressly refuses its own offerings or packages it helped prepare. TES proposes unconflicted reviewers for packages its team helped build, and EY argues that a client relationship alone need not preclude work. Government acceptance cannot be inferred from those proposals.

Reviewers decide a KSI is demonstrated only when evidence shows the outcome is both in place and working, traced to an authoritative source. We use these terms: • Security assertion: the provider's claim that an outcome is met. • Security measure: the specific mechanism or configuration that delivers it. • Evidence of implementation: artifacts showing the measure exists and is configured as claimed. • Evidence of effectiveness: data showing the measure works over time, including failures and how they were handled. • Verification: checking that evidence matches the stated requirement and the provider's own claims (did they build it as specified?). • Validation: confirming the measure actually achieves the security outcome in the real environment (does it solve the problem?). • Independent verification and validation (IV&V): verification and validation performed by a party independent of the provider, such as a 3PAO or the Government.
