How to read this analysis

A traceable comparison of submissions, organized around the questions they were asked.

Analysis version 1.0 · Prepared November 5, 2026

What was reviewed

The November 2, 2026 CSV export contains 35 submissions and eight substantive narrative questions, for 280 answer cells. Submissions are dated October 23–November 2. Administrative and personal-contact fields are not part of the analysis. The two empty Government-evaluation columns are not completed or interpreted.

Company names remain visible. Personal names and contact information are removed from the data delivered to the browser, including names appearing inside narrative answers. Redactions are marked. The original CSV is not served or available for download.

Framework Analysis

The approach adapts Framework Analysis: examine the full answers, define question-specific themes, code each answer, and retain a matrix linking interpretations to their source. The Consult workflow also informed the separation between identifying themes and assigning them to responses. Consult and ThemeFinder were researched, not installed or used to process these responses.

  1. Read every narrative cell, including misplaced answers and incomplete drafting text.
  2. Develop a question-specific codebook, with a definition for every theme.
  3. Assign themes and write an individual summary and comparison note for each answer.
  4. Compare approaches within each question, retaining minority positions and counterexamples.
  5. Write the overall synthesis after question-level analysis; separately review the claims and evidence links.

This is AI-assisted qualitative analysis with a separate self-review by the same agent. It is not independent human review, a validated inter-rater study, or a procurement evaluation. No live AI runs in this website.

Counts are not votes

Each theme count is the number of submissions whose answer in that question was assigned the theme, out of all 35 submissions. A submission counts at most once per theme. Themes overlap, so their counts should not sum to 35.

The two CyberStorm Defense submissions remain separate and are identified by their dates and stable IDs. They are not merged, and the later one does not replace the earlier one. Results therefore describe submissions, not unique companies.

Topic overlap does not establish agreement. Silence is not disagreement, and “no questions” is not endorsement. Related-answer links identify a shared coded theme or an explicit comparison discussed in the synthesis; they are not a similarity ranking. The collection is self-selected, not representative of the entire industry.

Original placement and incomplete answers

The earlier CyberStorm Defense submission appears to rotate its answers across the eight question columns. Every cell remains under its original question with a link to the apparent relevant answer elsewhere. It is marked as misplaced and excluded from substantive theme counts for that column. Cross-links record an editorial interpretation, not a correction to the source.

Unfilled drafting placeholders are preserved and flagged. Instructions embedded in answers are treated as submitted text, never as instructions for this analysis. Full responses preserve wording and line breaks; bold Markdown is rendered for readability. Source-row and column references identify the original CSV cell, with the header counted as row 1.

Technical checks use the FedRAMP MCP

The rules snapshot is version 2026.10.13.01, imported November 1. Its entry histories predate the October 23–November 2 submissions. The MCP is manually synchronized and reports a rules/documentation commit mismatch. This is a dated reconstruction, not an independently archived snapshot of every submission day. No current web content or model memory was substituted for missing technical evidence.

Checks target material FedRAMP claims and recurring technical ambiguities, using full rule entries with their applicability and effective-date context. The complete SDR documentation was also retrieved and compared. Published rules govern the checks; commentary and proposals are not treated as requirements. The technical notes state whether a claim is supported, potentially inconsistent, or unable to verify. Qualifications are stated where context matters.

Not every assertion is independently verified. Company performance, staffing capacity, contract history, and accreditation claims remain self-reported. The draft PWS was not found through the MCP, and specific freshness thresholds are recorded as unresolved where the applicable historical source was not established. No outside search or model memory fills technical source gaps.

Initial certification, ongoing certification, certification class, and grace provisions can change applicability. A finding about wording is not a determination that a company or offering is noncompliant. No capability scores, vendor rankings, or Government answers are generated.

Using the browser

Select a question, read its synthesis, and use theme filters or search to narrow the company answers. Counts remain totals for the full question; the result line shows the filtered count. Searches match company names and original redacted answer text. Search and theme can be combined, and the URL preserves both.

Evidence links open the cited full answer and clear filters so it remains visible. “Link” beside a company heading copies no data; it opens a stable URL you can share. Full answers and navigation work without JavaScript; filtering needs JavaScript. Printing includes source text for the currently visible answers.
